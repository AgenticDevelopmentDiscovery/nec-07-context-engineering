## A complete example

> One realistic case, carried from start to finish. The walkthroughs in
> § Content each isolate one mechanism; this is where the reader sees them
> working together, in the order they would actually be used.
>
> Show the real artifacts — the commands, the code, the output — not a
> description of them. State the starting situation and the result, so the
> reader can tell what the example achieved.

## A contrasting example

> A second case that differs from the first in a way that matters: another
> setting, another scale, another constraint. Say what changed and what stayed
> the same.
>
> One example teaches the example; two teach the pattern. The contrast is how
> the reader learns which parts are essential and which were incidental to the
> first case.

Replace this paragraph.

## Try it yourself

> An exercise the reader can do with what the tutorial has given them. State
> the task and what a correct result looks like, so they can check their own
> work.
>
> Keep it close to the examples above but not identical — one step the reader
> has to work out alone. It should need nothing the tutorial did not teach.
