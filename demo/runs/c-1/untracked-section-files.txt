## A contrasting example

> A second case chosen because it differs from the first in a way that matters —
> a different setting, scale, or constraint. Say what changed and what the tool
> did differently because of it.
>
> One example teaches the example; two that differ teach the tool. If the second
> only repeats the first, cut it.

Replace this paragraph.

## An example that goes wrong

> A case where one of the § Pitfalls actually happens: what the reader would
> see, how to recognise it, and the repair. Show the failing output, then the
> fixed one.
>
> Pitfalls described in the abstract are easy to nod along to. One watched end
> to end is the one the reader remembers when it happens to them.

Replace this paragraph.
