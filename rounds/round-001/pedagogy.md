# Pedagogical Readiness — Round 1

**Recommendation:** needs revision
the claims are sound and honestly sourced, but the declared reader is told what the four capabilities are and shown a result, not shown how to do any of them — no index on the page, no command to rerun, no worked diagnosis.

**Audience read against:** Graduate students from mixed engineering and science disciplines, one month into an agentic-development course. They have Claude Code installed, have run it in a git repository under a permission model, and have edited a `CLAUDE.md` once. They know tokens and a finite budget, not attention. `metadata.yaml` promises them one outcome above all: to *diagnose a context failure in their own session and fix it* by changing what the agent sees or when. The audience field is specific and usable; it is not the problem.

## Strengths
- The audience, the proposal and the prose agree on the promise. The four capabilities appear word for word in `topic.md`, in `01-context.prose.md` § What this tutorial covers, and in `04-conclusion.prose.md` § What you can do now, each mapped there to the unit that is supposed to deliver it. The reader always knows what they are being taught.
- Evidence is graded on the page, which a graduate reader will respect: "that is advice, not evidence", "a vendor report", "No source we found tests either effect in agentic coding", "Post hoc, not pre-registered". The null result is reported as one ("Our prediction that the arms would separate failed").
- The spines convert. Almost every claim in the four `.concepts.md` files has a matching line of prose, and what was not run is marked *Not run* rather than written as a result. There is no spine growing ahead of the prose.
- `03-content.prose.md` § Layers and § On demand teach distinctions the reader can act on and would not guess: instruction files are "concatenated, not overriding"; `@path` imports are front-loaded, so "Moving text into imports saves nothing"; `--add-dir` "gives access and loads nothing".
- WHAT before WHY holds, and `01-context.prose.md` § What context engineering is defines the thing in two short paragraphs with no unnamed prerequisite.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index, not a manual — capability 2 is "Write a `CLAUDE.md` that works as an index", and no index appears anywhere in the document. The reader gets three nouns ("commands, conventions that cannot be inferred from the files, and pointers") and a line count ("A 32-line index"). The file exists at `demo/index.CLAUDE.md` and the prose neither shows a line of it nor points to it. A reader who has edited a `CLAUDE.md` once cannot write an index from a description of one; this is the capability the tutorial is named for and it is the one least illustrated.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo / § What changed — the second half of capability 2 is "test on a fixed prompt, over repeated runs", and the procedure is not on the page. "Five per arm, headless" is the whole method: no command (`demo/run.sh` uses `claude -p`), no statement of how "reads before the first edit" or "tokens at the first edit" were counted, no pointer to `demo/PROTOCOL.md` or `demo/run.sh`. The reader sees the result of a test and is credited in the conclusion with the ability to run one.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls and `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` § What you can do now — diagnosis, the outcome `metadata.yaml` promises, is delivered as three symptom bullets and one sentence ("when the agent errs, ask what it could see, and when"). No failure is ever diagnosed on the page: every demo run was correct, so the tutorial never shows a session going wrong, `/context` or the transcript being read, the cause located, and the fix applied. The spine asks this itself (`04-conclusion.concepts.md`: "Enough for the reader to do it in their own session?"). The answer on current evidence is no.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails and `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo — the control is never said. "a convention written nowhere was followed in 0 of 15 runs" arrives one section before the demo is introduced, and the demo slide lists only "one control". The reader cannot tell what the convention was (it is "Nothing between `#` and first `##`", `demo/RESULTS.md` line 18), so the only observed *too little* failure in the tutorial reads as a tautology rather than a case. The observables list is also unnumbered while the next slide refers to "Observables 1 to 5".
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What `/context` shows at launch — capability 1 is "say what each part costs", and the slide is a figure with a caption that explains one row of nine. The two largest rows, "Autocompact buffer" (33.0k) and "System tools" (17.4k), are never named in the prose. More seriously, the figure shows 3% of the window used and Memory files at 0.4%, and the tutorial never reconciles that with "every line is paid for in every session" or with a headline saving of 46.6k against 41.3k tokens. A reader looking at 93.5% free space will conclude the index does not matter, and the prose gives them no reason to think otherwise. The lesson of the null result is in the spine ("a self-describing repository needs less in `CLAUDE.md`", `03-content.concepts.md`) and not in the prose.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — load is inverted. Three of eight units go to a demo whose behavioural finding is null; § Layers and § On demand, which carry capabilities 3 and 4 and the most new vocabulary, get one bullet slide each and no instance. There is no example of two conflicting rules and the conflict removed, and none of "for a given file" being decided. Terms used without a gloss and not covered by the declared audience: "managed policy", "path-scoped rules", "a skill's body", "auto memory", "a hook or a permission setting", "headless", "Arms".
- `topic.md` — the proposal has fallen behind the tutorial. Its Open questions still list "The exact demo prompt and the number of runs per arm" and what is loaded in both arms, all settled in `03-content.concepts.md`. Its Scope says "the tutorial uses percentages, not token counts" (repeated as a Decision in `03-content.concepts.md`), while the prose reports 47.2k, 46.6k and 41.3k and no percentage. Its document-length question is undecided, and the consequence is visible: `02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails and all of `03-content.prose.md` after the first unit are slide bullets, so the long-form document is the deck without the speaker.

## Actionable
1. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index, not a manual — show the index: six to ten lines excerpted from `demo/index.CLAUDE.md`, one each of a command, a non-inferable convention and a pointer, with the path to the full file. Pay for the room by cutting the "sufficient statistic" bullet or the "under 200 lines" bullet. (*why:* delivers capability 2's first half; the largest single step from needs revision toward minor polish, for the least work)
2. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo: one prompt, two windows — number the observables 1 to 6, state the control in words ("nothing between the `#` heading and the first `##`, written nowhere in the template"), and add one line giving the run command and the pointer: `claude -p` on `demo/prompt.txt`, script at `demo/run.sh`, protocol at `demo/PROTOCOL.md`. (*why:* makes the test reproducible by the reader and makes "Observables 1 to 5" and "the control" checkable; follow-along)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls: symptom, then the structural fix — replace the three parallel bullets with one worked diagnosis taken from the recorded history leak (`demo/runs/superseded-a-1-history-leak`): the symptom, what was checked, what was found reachable, what was changed. Keep the other two as one line each. (*why:* the declared audience outcome is diagnosis; this is the only real failure the team has on record, and it is currently one clause)
4. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What `/context` shows at launch — add two or three lines reading the figure: what the largest rows are, which rows the reader controls (Memory files, Skills) and which they do not, and one sentence on why 0.4% of the window is still worth cutting, or an admission that on this window it barely is. Move the spine's lesson ("a self-describing repository needs less in `CLAUDE.md`") into § What changed, and what did not. (*why:* delivers capability 1 and closes the gap a careful reader will open against the central recommendation)
5. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Layers and § On demand — add one concrete instance to each: a pair of contradicting rules in user and project files with the conflict removed; one named file from this repository decided as front-load or on demand by the stated rule. Gloss "path-scoped rules", "managed policy" and "auto memory" in a clause each, or cut them. (*why:* capabilities 3 and 4 are asserted, not illustrated; also removes the unnamed prerequisites)
6. `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails — say what the unwritten convention was, or move the demo sentence out and point forward to § What changed. (*why:* the first evidence for the *too little* failure is currently unreadable without the demo)
7. `topic.md` § Scope and § Open questions — move the proposal: strike the resolved open questions, and settle "percentages, not token counts" one way, in the proposal and in the prose together. Decide the document-length question; the items above add prose that belongs in the document and may not fit a slide, which is the case for `::: notes` blocks. (*why:* the panel holds the tutorial to the proposal; a stale one costs every later round)
