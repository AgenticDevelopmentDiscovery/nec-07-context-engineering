# Clarity & Exposition — Round 1

**Recommendation:** needs revision
the through-line is sound and the hedging is exact, but the demo at the centre of the tutorial cannot be followed from the prose alone: its control is never defined, its observables are written in this repository's private vocabulary, and the lesson of its null result is stated only in the spine

## Strengths
- The thesis is stated once, early, and paid off at the end. `sections/01-context.prose.md` says context engineering "acts on what the model can see, not on the wording of the request"; `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls closes on "Every fix changes what is loaded, or when. None is a rewording." A single-pass reader leaves with the one sentence the tutorial is about.
- The order what -> why -> how -> what else holds, and the roadmap in § What this tutorial covers ("a readable budget, a demo on this repository, the index, layers, on-demand loading, pitfalls") matches the seven `##` units of `03-content` in order. Nothing arrives unannounced.
- The capability list is identical in § What this tutorial covers and § What you can do now, and the second copy carries a pointer to the unit that delivered each item. The repetition is earned because it lets the reader audit the promise.
- Claims are scoped to what their source tested, in the sentence that makes them: "[@hong2025, a vendor report ...]", "An earlier study found its models ...", "that is advice, not evidence", "Post hoc, not pre-registered". The reader is never left to guess the strength of a claim.
- The null result is reported as one ("Our prediction that the arms would separate failed") rather than argued around. This is the most credible passage in the document.
- § On demand adds a third state, "Reachable only", to the binary the capability list sets up, and the heading ("reachable is not loaded") names the distinction. § Pitfalls then reuses it. That is a term introduced where it is needed and used again where it pays.
- The spines are in note form throughout, record what was rejected as well as what was chosen, and point into `demo/` for every number. The planned order of argument is coherent.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — "one control" is listed among the observables in § The demo and never defined. § What changed then reports "the control failed in 5 of 5 in both", one sentence before "Our prediction ... failed". A reader cannot tell that a failing control is the expected outcome, because the prose never says the control is a convention written nowhere in the repository. The only gloss is in `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` ("a convention written nowhere was followed in 0 of 15 runs"), which does not call it the control and does not say what the convention was.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — the observables in § The demo are in the template's vocabulary: "Both files of the section created; the sidecar in note form; numeric order". The prose never says what this repository is, what a section pair is, or what a sidecar is. The declared audience has edited a `CLAUDE.md`; nothing says they have seen this template.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § What changed refers to "Observables 1 to 5", but the list in § The demo is an unnumbered run of seven items separated by semicolons. The reader has to count to find out which five are meant, and that "one control" is the sixth.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § What changed ends on a post-hoc observation and draws no conclusion. The lesson exists only in `sections/03-content.concepts.md` ("a self-describing repository needs less in `CLAUDE.md`"; "The argument rests on cost"). The slide that follows a null result is where the reader most needs to be told what to take from it.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — scale is never confronted. The figure under § What `/context` shows at launch reads 32.3k of 1.0M tokens, 3% used, 93.5% free. The index argument in § CLAUDE.md as an index rests on 41.3k against 46.6k, a difference of about half a percent of that window. The prose says "every line is paid for in every session" but never says why a saving of that size matters. The figure and the argument are on adjacent slides and pull in opposite directions.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` and `topic.md` — `topic.md` § Scope says "the tutorial uses percentages, not token counts" and the spine of § The window is a budget you can read decides "Percentages, not token counts". The prose body gives no percentage anywhere and reports every cost in tokens (47.2k, 46.6k, 41.3k, 4.2k). The same file is also measured in three units: "240-line", "1,854 words", "4.2k".
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § The window is a budget you can read says `/context` "draws current usage as a grid" and that the launch cost comes from "instruction files, auto memory and skill descriptions". The figure that follows is a table under a bar, and its two largest rows are "System tools" (17.4k) and "Autocompact buffer" (33.0k), neither of which the prose names. Capability 1 is "say what each part costs", and the text never reads the figure.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § CLAUDE.md as an index: "The documentation's target is "under 200 lines", as advice, not enforcement: the file arrives "as a user message after the system prompt"". Two claims are fused. As written, "advice, not enforcement" describes the 200-line target; the clause after the colon shows it was meant to describe the file's contents. The reader cannot recover which.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § CLAUDE.md as an index: "This template ships a 240-line manual." stands as a bare fact. The spine decides "Tension named openly ... and the template defends that choice". The prose names neither the tension nor the defence.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § The demo is headed "one prompt, two windows" and then says "Five per arm" of three arms, with "Arm C ... is reported on the index slide". Arm C's result then arrives two units later as "its worst run below the best of either other arm", which requires the reader to have kept 44.7k in mind across a slide. "The index slide" is also a slide reference in a source that renders to a document and a website.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § What changed labels the arms "without the file" and "with the manual" in one bullet and "B" and "A" in another. Two naming schemes for two arms on one slide.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § CLAUDE.md as an index: "Formally, an index aims to be a *sufficient statistic*". The spine calls this an analogy that "holds for choosing what to load, not for the contents". "Formally" claims more than the spine intends.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § Layers gives the load order in two bullets and then says conflict "has no documented winner". Capability 3 asks the reader to "know the load order"; the prose never says what knowing it is for once it has been shown not to decide a conflict.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § On demand lists five mechanisms in one line ("The agent's own file reads, `@file` typed in a prompt, a subdirectory `CLAUDE.md`, path-scoped rules, a skill's body") without saying what triggers each. Capability 4 is a decision "for a given file"; the unit gives the rule and a mechanism example (`/round`) but no file decided.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — jargon used without introduction: "headless" (§ The demo), "path-scoped rules" (§ On demand), "managed policy" (§ Layers), "a hook or a permission setting", "a superseded run", "a dry run" (§ Pitfalls).
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` — § How a window fails, "**Caveat.** No source we found tests either effect": "either" follows three bullets (too little, too much, position). It means length and position; the reader has to work that out. "**The bound.**" is a label that does not say what is bounded; the sentence under it is the test for when the tutorial does not apply.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` — "In our demo, a convention written nowhere was followed in 0 of 15 runs" cites a demo the reader has met only as one phrase in the roadmap, with no forward reference to where it is described.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` — the closing paragraph ("The name is recent ...") is a fact with no connection to the two paragraphs above it. The spine gives it a job ("It explains why the problem changed when agents became multi-step"); the prose does not do that job.
- `sections/02-motivation.concepts.md` — the heading is "Two ways a window fails"; the prose heading is "How a window fails". The spine's own header says each `##` is one `##` of the prose. `sections/04-conclusion.concepts.md` also refers to "§ Two ways a window fails".
- `topic.md` — the proposal no longer matches the document. § Scope groups "`@path` imports and `--add-dir`" as front-loaded; the prose says `--add-dir` "loads nothing". § Shape describes "a throwaway git worktree" and "a ~30-line index"; the prose reports single-commit copies and a 32-line index. § Open questions still lists "The exact demo prompt and the number of runs per arm", which the prose has answered.
- `sections/03-content.concepts.md` — decisions recorded and not realized in prose: "Side-by-side diff of outputs, not transcripts" (no diff appears), "Figure = annotated `/context` reading" (the figure is a caption, not an annotation), and the two named above (the lesson of the null result, the tension over the 240-line manual). These are four intentions the spine treats as settled and the prose has not delivered.

## Actionable
1. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo: one prompt, two windows — number the observables 1 to 7, say in one clause what the repository is (a tutorial template whose sections are pairs of files), and define the control where it is listed: a convention stated nowhere in the repository, expected to fail in every arm. In § What changed, replace "the control failed in 5 of 5 in both" with wording that marks the failure as the expected outcome. In `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails, name the same thing the same way and add a forward reference to § The demo. (*why:* the demo is the tutorial's only first-hand evidence, and as written the reader cannot tell what was measured or which failures were predicted)
2. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What changed, and what did not — end the unit with the lesson the spine already records: on a repository that describes itself, the file changed the cost of reaching the answer, not the answer. Pay for the sentence by cutting or shortening the post-hoc bullet. (*why:* a null result with no stated consequence reads as a failed demo; one sentence turns it into the premise of the next unit)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index, not a manual — report the three arms' cost at first edit as a share of the window alongside or instead of token counts, and say in one sentence why a difference of that size matters when the launch figure shows 93.5% free. Settle on one unit for the size of `CLAUDE.md`. Then either change `topic.md` § Scope and the spine decision "Percentages, not token counts", or honour them. (*why:* the reader's first question after the figure is whether any of this is scarce; the prose is silent, and the promised unit is the one that would answer it)
4. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index, not a manual — split the fused sentence into two claims: the 200-line target is the documentation's advice; the file is delivered as a user message, so its contents are advice to the model and not enforced. State the tension over the 240-line template in one clause or cut the sentence. Move the Arm C numbers into § What changed as one three-row table (arm, reads, cost at first edit) so all three arms are compared in one place, and replace "the index slide" with the heading. (*why:* removes an ambiguity in the unit's main claim, and stops the reader carrying numbers across two slides to make a comparison)
5. `topic.md` § Scope and § Shape — move `--add-dir` out of the front-loaded group, replace the worktree description with the single-commit copies actually used, and close the open questions the prose has answered. In the same pass, rename the heading in `sections/02-motivation.concepts.md` and the reference in `sections/04-conclusion.concepts.md` to "How a window fails". (*why:* the proposal and the spines are what the document is held to; where they disagree with the prose, a reader of both cannot tell which is current)
