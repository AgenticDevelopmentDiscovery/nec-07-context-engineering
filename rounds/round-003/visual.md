## Strengths
- **Presentation.** The budget is held as declared: 14 content frames split 2, 2, 8, 2, one divider per section (pp. 2, 5, 8, 17), no stray frame from content between `#` and the first `##`, no references frame.
- **Presentation and document together.** The `::: notes` mechanism does what it was adopted for. § What changed, and what did not is a table and one paragraph on p. 12, and the same unit in `output/document.pdf` (p. 4) carries four bullets of detail between them, in the right place, without a second source.
- **All three registers.** Table 1 is the right form for a null result: two columns that are identical down every row show "nothing differed" faster than a sentence does. It renders intact in the deck, the document and the site (where it scrolls inside itself).
- **All three registers.** The `/context` capture survives rendering: 256 ppi on the slide (p. 10), 185 ppi in the document (p. 3), row labels and token counts legible at both. This answers the spine's open question in `03-content.concepts.md` § What `/context` shows at launch: it does not need redrawing as SVG for sharpness.
- **Presentation.** The index excerpt on p. 13 shows the artefact rather than describing it, fits the frame after re-breaking, and discloses what was omitted.
- **Website.** Cross-references name the heading rather than a number. That is the register-neutral choice, because the site prints no "Figure 1" or "Table 1" and no section numbers.
- **Website and document.** The four capabilities are word for word the same in § What this tutorial covers and § What you can do now, and the second carries a § pointer per capability, so a reader who lands on the conclusion can find the unit that delivers each one.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — **the index result is never read out on a slide.** Row C of the table is on p. 12, but the paragraph under it speaks only of "the manual". The sentence that reads row C ("The index matched the manual at the lowest cost of the three") is inside a `::: notes` block under § CLAUDE.md as an index, so it is absent from p. 13. The unit titled "an index, not a manual" projects a load claim and an excerpt, and no result. The deck's main claim rests on a table row the audience must interpret alone.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — **the figure cannot show what its caption says.** The caption on p. 10 gives "32.5k spent". That number is in the header of the full readout, which the crop removes; the seven visible rows sum to 32.4k as displayed. A viewer who looks for 32.5k in the figure will not find it. The previous unit says `/context` shows "a bar and a table by category" and "lists which instruction and memory files loaded"; the crop keeps one of those three and removes the bar and the file list (both present in `demo/runs/context-at-launch-full.png`).
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — **the figure's relational content is in the notes, not in the figure.** Who controls which rows (the harness: System prompt, System tools) and which rows are counted against which are reserved is the reading that makes the table teach. On the slide the notes do not exist, so p. 10 is an unannotated list of nine rows. The largest numbers on it, 934.5k free and 93.5%, argue against the premise of a scarce budget, and nothing on that frame answers them; the answer is two frames later.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — **no figure for the central abstraction.** The definition in § What context engineering is turns on "what goes in, when". § The window is a budget you can read, § CLAUDE.md as an index, § On demand and § Pitfalls each state part of a timing relation (loads at launch; loads on use; re-read after compaction; lost at compaction) and no figure assembles them. The spine asks whether a timeline "earns its place, or restates the two lists" (`03-content.concepts.md` § On demand, Open questions). It earns its place: the lists give membership, and the missing relation is mechanism against moment of entry, including the fact that compaction behaves like a second launch for files and not for conversation.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo — **heading and body disagree on the frame.** p. 11 is titled "one prompt, two windows" and lists three arms. The spine keeps the heading on the ground that "this unit compares A with B; C is reported under § CLAUDE.md as an index" (`03-content.concepts.md` § The demo). The prose no longer does that: it says C is "reported in the table under § What changed". The spine is stale and the heading rests on it.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo — **p. 11 is at capacity** (12 lines, 134 words; first line at 42 pt, last at 225 pt, no room for another line). The observables bullet is a seven-item list written as one sentence, with bare numerals running into the text ("5 no commit; 6, the control"). It is genuinely dense rather than padded, but it is a list in the wrong form for projection, and its seven items do not match the three column groups the next frame uses.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails — **p. 7 is at capacity** (12 lines, 150 words, the most on any frame). Four ideas under one heading. The last clause of the Position bullet ("none of these effects is tested in agentic coding in any source we found") is repeated as the first bullet of § Open edges (p. 19).
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What changed, and what did not — **p. 12 is tight and does not stand alone.** "Control" and "Reads" are column headers defined only on the previous frame and in the notes.
- `sections/*.prose.md` — **the document register has no connective tissue.** Ten of the fourteen units are bullet lists in `output/document.pdf`, and no unit opens by saying why it follows the one before. The move from § What `/context` shows at launch to § The demo, and from § CLAUDE.md as an index to § Layers, is a cold start in all three outputs. The notes blocks add detail; none adds a transition. The long-form read is currently the deck with annotations.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo — in the document (p. 4) and the site, the two notes blocks split one list into three, so "Question" and "Runs" sit apart from the four bullets between them with visibly different spacing.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index — the fenced block is highlighted as Markdown, so on p. 13 the commands (`just build`, `just doc`) are pale teal italic, the faintest text on the frame, and they are the content the unit is about. In both PDFs the em dashes of the excerpt render in the monospaced face as hyphens; the site shows them as written.
- `sections/*.prose.md` — **`#` headings do not orient.** "Context" is a section title inside a tutorial about context, on divider p. 2 and at the top of the site sidebar; "Content" names nothing. A reader who lands on the site from a search result sees "Content › Layers" and learns only the second half. All seven § references are plain text in `_site/index.html`, not links.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` and `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` — headings and references set `/context` in code and `CLAUDE.md` in plain type (p. 10 against p. 13; both forms in one list on p. 18).
- `sections/04-conclusion.concepts.md` — the spine maps capability 1 to § The window is a budget you can read; the prose maps it to § What `/context` shows at launch.
- `_site/style.css` — the light-theme capture is a bright panel under the dark colour scheme. Low priority.
