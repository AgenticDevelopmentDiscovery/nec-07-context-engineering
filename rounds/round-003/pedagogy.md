# Pedagogical Readiness — Round 3

**Recommendation:** needs revision
the evidence is honest and the structure is sound, but three of the four capabilities and the declared outcome (diagnose and fix in one's own session) are stated as lists rather than shown once, so the reader can repeat the rules and cannot yet perform them

**Audience read against:** Graduate students from mixed engineering and science disciplines, one month into an agentic-development course. They have installed Claude Code, run it in a git repository under a permission model, and edited a `CLAUDE.md` once. They know tokens and a finite budget; they are not assumed to know attention, and nothing in the declaration says they know skills, hooks, auto memory, rules files, MCP or headless mode. The standard they set for the tutorial is a behaviour: diagnose a context failure in their own session and fix it by changing what the agent sees or when.

## Strengths
- The audience declaration is specific and the document's level matches it. Nothing on the page needs attention mechanics, and the four capabilities are stated in the same words in `topic.md`, § What this tutorial covers and § What you can do now, each mapped to the unit that delivers it.
- The demo is reported the way a graduate reader should be taught to report one: observables fixed before the runs, a control, a failed prediction named as failed, inference separated from measurement ("we infer, and did not measure"), post-hoc observations labelled. This is the strongest teaching in the document and it is teaching by example.
- Capability 1 is delivered. The `/context` figure, its caption and the notes under it let the reader run the command in a fresh session and check their own readout against a real one, row by row.
- The spines are converting, not growing. Open questions are being closed or moved with a reason, numbers in the prose trace to `demo/RESULTS.md`, and the limits of the demo are stated in § Open edges rather than left for the reader to discover.
- The `::: notes` mechanism works where it is used: §§ What `/context` shows at launch, What changed, and CLAUDE.md as an index read as a document, not as a slide transcript.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` (§ Pitfalls) and `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` (§ What you can do now) — diagnosis, the outcome the audience is declared to walk away with, is three symptom bullets and one sentence ("ask what it could see, and when"). There is no procedure and no worked case: the reader is never shown how to establish what the agent could see (the per-file Memory files block of `/context`, the reads in the transcript), and that block is cropped out of the only figure. The decision rule is also inconsistent on the page: § How a window fails says that if the agent had the information and still erred "it is not a context problem", while the first pitfall is exactly that case (a rule in a loaded `CLAUDE.md` is ignored) and prescribes a context fix. A reader applying the bound would stop before reaching the fix. The spine for 04 asks whether this is enough for the reader to do it in their own session; on the page as it stands, it is not.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` (§ The demo; § What changed) — the only failure the demo observed is never fixed. The control was missed in 0 of 15 runs because the rule was in no window; the tutorial's thesis is that such a failure is fixed by changing what the agent sees; and no run puts the rule in the window to show the count move. The reader leaves with a demonstration that a `CLAUDE.md` changed no behaviour and with no demonstration that one can. Beside that, the case for the index rests on about 5k tokens against a window the figure shows as 1.0M with 93.5% free, and "cost on any window" does not answer the question the figure invites. The documented reason to keep the file short (adherence, first pitfall) is never connected to the index unit.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` (§ Layers; § On demand) — capabilities 3 and 4 are taught as taxonomy with no instance. § Layers never gives the file locations of the user, project and local layers, so "place an instruction in the right layer" cannot be carried out from the page; no conflict is shown, so "remove it" has nothing to act on. § On demand names eight mechanisms in five bullets (`@path` imports, typed `@file`, subdirectory `CLAUDE.md`, path-scoped rules, skill bodies, `--add-dir`, commands, subagents) without the syntax or location of any, and the one example ("Here") is a skill the reader did not place. Capability 4 says "decide, for a given file"; no file is decided. These are the densest units in the document and the only content units without a `::: notes` block: the hardest material has the least room.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` (§ The demo) — capability 2 promises the reader can "test on a fixed prompt, over repeated runs"; the page gives the design and not the means. "Headless" is one word in the notes; there is no command, no statement of how reads and tokens were counted, and no pointer to `demo/run.sh`, `demo/table.sh` or `demo/prompt.txt`. The two precautions that make the test valid (copies with no shared git history, auto memory stripped) appear only as a pitfall, not as setup. The document and site carry no repository address, so the `demo/` paths the prose does cite lead nowhere for a reader holding the PDF.
- `sections/01-context.prose.md`, `sections/03-content.prose.md` — assumed knowledge that is neither supplied nor named in the audience: auto memory (used from the first paragraph), skills and skill bodies, hooks, path-scoped rules, managed policy, MCP (two rows of the figure), "sidecar" in observable 2. Each is a clause to gloss; together they decide whether a reader from outside computing follows § On demand at all.
- `topic.md` against the prose — two promises are not on the page. The proposal lists `/compact` among the concrete instruments and the prose never names it; the proposal and the 03 spine promise results "as a recorded diff with counts" and the prose gives counts only, so the reader never sees what a control violation looks like in a file the agent wrote.

## Actionable
1. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Layers: order is documented, conflict is not — add a `::: notes` block giving the path of each layer (user, project, local; managed policy named only) and one worked conflict in two lines: the two contradicting rules, where each lives, which one is deleted. Costs no slide. (*why:* makes capability 3 performable from the page; needs revision → minor polish on this unit)
2. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls: symptom, then the structural fix — add a `::: notes` block with the diagnostic steps in order: read `/context` and its per-file list to see what loaded; check the transcript for what was read; then sort the failure into not loaded, loaded and lost, loaded and ignored, or reachable and unintended. Reconcile with § How a window fails by rewording the bound so that "loaded but ignored" is inside it or explicitly outside it, not both. (*why:* delivers the declared outcome; the single largest readiness gain)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What changed, and what did not — close the loop on the control. Either run a further arm with the index plus the one-line rule and report the count, labelled as added after the fact, or state in the notes that the fix was applied to this repository's `CLAUDE.md` and has not been measured. Do not write a result before the run. (*why:* gives the thesis one positive instance; without it the demo teaches only that the file did not matter)
4. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § On demand: reachable is not loaded — add a `::: notes` block that decides two real files from this repository, one front-loaded and one deferred, with the reason for each, and gives one line of syntax or location for `@path` imports, path-scoped rules and a subdirectory `CLAUDE.md`. If the slide is over budget, move `--add-dir` detail into the notes. (*why:* converts capability 4 from a list to a decision; relieves the heaviest unit)
5. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo: one prompt, two windows — extend the Runs note: the headless invocation in one line, the definition of "tokens at first edit", the two setup precautions (no shared history, auto memory stripped), and pointers to `demo/run.sh`, `demo/table.sh` and `demo/prompt.txt`, with a statement that the scripts carry the author's paths. Give the repository address once, in § What this tutorial covers notes. (*why:* makes the "test" half of capability 2 followable)
6. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index, not a manual — in the notes, answer the scale question directly: say what 5k tokens is and is not against a 1.0M window, and tie the case for a short file to the documented adherence claim already cited in § Pitfalls, marked as documentation, not measurement. Name two or three things the manual held that the index dropped, so the reader sees a cut being made. (*why:* the index argument currently survives only if the reader does not look at the figure)
7. `sections/01-context.prose.md` § What context engineering is and `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What `/context` shows at launch — gloss auto memory, skill, hook, MCP and "sidecar" in a clause each at first use, in notes; or add them to the `audience` in `metadata.yaml` if the course has taught them by week four. (*why:* removes unnamed prerequisites for the mixed-discipline reader)
8. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What changed, and what did not — add to the notes a three- or four-line excerpt from one run's new prose file showing the text the agent placed between `#` and the first `##`, with the run named. (*why:* makes the control concrete and delivers the "recorded diff" the proposal promised)
9. `topic.md` § What it is — the proposal should move: drop `/compact` from the instrument list, or add one clause naming it beside compaction in § The window is a budget you can read. State which. (*why:* keeps the panel from holding the document to an instrument it does not teach)
