# Pedagogical Readiness — Round 4

**Recommendation:** needs revision
two of the promised outcomes, testing a `CLAUDE.md` and diagnosing a failure, are reported as results the author obtained and not given as procedures the declared reader can carry out from the page.

**Audience read against:** Graduate students from mixed engineering and science disciplines, one month into an agentic-development course. They have run Claude Code in a git repository under a permission model and edited a `CLAUDE.md` once; they know tokens and a finite budget, and nothing about attention. `metadata.yaml` grants them no knowledge of auto memory, skills, MCP, hooks, subagents or headless mode. The outcome they are promised is to diagnose a context failure in their own session and fix it by changing what the agent sees or when.

## Strengths
- The audience is declared precisely and the level matches it. Nothing assumes attention mechanics, and the definition (weights against window) is on the first slide in words this reader has.
- The four capabilities are stated in the same words at the opening and the close, and each is mapped to the unit that delivers it. The reader can check the promise against the delivery without help.
- The demo is reported as a null result, with its failed prediction, its unexplained observable (5) and its limits stated. For graduate students this teaches how to treat evidence as well as what the evidence was, and the literature in § How a window fails is dated and scoped to what each study tested.
- § Layers is followable as written: load order, a placement rule in four cases, file locations in the notes, and one conflict worked to its fix.
- The `::: notes` blocks put the detail a solitary reader needs (paths, where each mechanism is written, the observables enumerated) in the document and the site without costing a slide.
- The spine records decisions with what was rejected, and its Not doing lists hold the scope in `topic.md`. Intentions have been converted: arm C was run before its capability was claimed.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo, § What changed — capability 2 promises the reader can "test on a fixed prompt, over repeated runs, whether it changed the agent's behaviour". The prose gives the design and the outcome and none of the procedure: no command (the notes say "headless" and stop), no statement of how a run was scored, no pointer to `demo/PROTOCOL.md`, `demo/RESULTS.md` or `demo/run.sh`. The two pointers into `demo/` that do appear cannot be followed from the PDF or the site, which carry no repository address and copy only `figures/`. A reader who wants to test their own file has a table to admire and nothing to run.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls; `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` § What you can do now — diagnosis is the outcome in `metadata.yaml`, and it is delivered as three symptom, cause, fix bullets and one closing sentence. No case is walked from symptom through a check to a fix, and the tutorial never shows a fix working: its only behavioural result is that the file changed nothing. The control is a diagnosed failure already in hand (followed in 0 of 15 runs, stated nowhere, contradicted by the existing sections) and it is left as a footnote to the motivation. It is also the one convention "the files cannot supply", so it is the best available example of a line that belongs in an index, and § CLAUDE.md as an index does not mention it. The spine asks the question itself (`sections/04-conclusion.concepts.md`, Open questions: "Enough for the reader to do it in their own session?") and has not answered it.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails against `sections/03-content.prose.md` — "too much" carries most of the motivation's citations and has no fix anywhere in the content except a shorter `CLAUDE.md`. The only `/context` reading is at launch, with Messages at 10 tokens and 93.5% free, so the reader never sees the window the motivation describes, one filled by file reads and tool output. `topic.md` promises reading the window "at a given moment" of a session and lists `/compact` among the instruments; the prose delivers the launch moment only and never names `/compact`. The tutorial should move, not the proposal: a reader sent to diagnose a long session needs the mid-session reading.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` throughout — assumed knowledge that is never named or glossed: auto memory (used in four units, and the cause in one pitfall), skill descriptions against skill bodies, MCP tools (two rows of the figure, not in the notes), hooks, subagents, headless, and `just`. Each is one clause to fix. Unglossed, the pitfall "acted on an auto memory saved in another directory" is unreadable to a reader who has only edited a `CLAUDE.md`.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § On demand — the densest unit in the document: about ten mechanisms under one heading, of which this reader has met perhaps two. The decision the capability names ("for a given file") is worked on the two easiest cases available, the index itself and the README. Neither is a file the reader would hesitate over.

## Actionable

(*why:* makes capability 2 followable; this alone moves the test half from reported to taught, and it is a notes edit with no new slide.)
2. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index, not a manual — add one notes bullet naming the control as the kind of line an index exists for: stated in no file, contradicted by the sections, followed in 0 of 15 runs. State that adding it was not tested. (*why:* turns "conventions the files cannot supply" from an assertion into a rule with a case; uses numbers already in `demo/RESULTS.md`.)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls: symptom, then the structural fix — give each pitfall its check before its fix. For the ignored rule: `/context` lists the memory files that loaded, which is the per-file block the figure crops out. Add the check in the notes and say where in the full readout it appears. (*why:* a symptom with two possible causes is not yet a diagnosis; this moves the declared outcome from needs revision toward minor polish.)
4. `sections/03-content.prose.md` first use of each term — gloss in one clause each: auto memory, skill (description against body), MCP tools, hook, subagent, headless, `just`.
