# Clarity & Exposition — Round 2

**Recommendation:** needs revision
the through-line holds end to end, but the central term is defined after it is used and off the slides, the demo's conclusion is stated twice before the demo is introduced, and the unit that delivers capability 1 contains no reading of its own figure

## Strengths
- The opening claim and the closing claim are the same claim. `01-context` says the practice "acts on what the model can see, not on the wording of the request"; § Pitfalls ends "Every fix changes what is loaded, or when. None is a rewording"; § What you can do now ends "ask what it could see, and when". A reader who remembers one sentence remembers the right one.
- The four capabilities appear word for word at both ends, and the conclusion maps each to the unit that delivered it. The reader can check the promise against the delivery without help.
- Headings state claims, not topics: "Layers: order is documented, conflict is not" and "On demand: reachable is not loaded" each tell the reader the point before the bullets do.
- The null result is reported as one. "On a repository that describes itself, the file changed the cost of reaching the answer, not the answer" is precise, short, and claims no more than the table shows. The sources in § How a window fails are likewise each cited only for what they tested, with the gap (agentic coding untested) said outright.
- § On demand sorts mechanisms into three groups (front-loaded, on demand, reachable only) rather than two, which is what makes `--add-dir` intelligible instead of an exception to memorise.
- The spines are in note form, record rejected alternatives with reasons, and are concrete enough to write from.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § What `/context` shows at launch has no prose of its own. The unit is a figure plus one notes sentence, "Memory files is this repository's `CLAUDE.md` (4.2k) and the auto-memory index (163)", which glosses one row and gives the second number no unit. Nothing tells the reader what to read off the panel: that 32.3k is spent before any prompt, which rows make up that figure, why the Autocompact buffer (33.0k) is larger than the total "used" and is not part of it, or which rows the reader controls. In the rendered document the figure floats to the page after the § What changed heading, so § 3.2 reads as that single orphan sentence about a panel the reader has not yet seen.
- `sections/03-content.concepts.md` — the claims for § What `/context` shows at launch (used at launch 32.3k; largest rows; Memory files decomposed) are the reading the prose lacks. They sit in the spine unrealized. The Decisions under § The window is a budget you can read still describe the figure as "annotated"; the figure shipped is a bare capture.
- `sections/01-context.prose.md` and `sections/03-content.prose.md` — "index" is the tutorial's central term and is never defined where the reader meets it. Capability 2 uses it bare ("a `CLAUDE.md` that works as an index"); `topic.md` carries the gloss inline ("commands, conventions that cannot be inferred from the files, pointers") and the prose dropped it. The definition first appears six units later, inside a `::: notes` block under § CLAUDE.md as an index, so it never reaches a slide. "Manual" is defined only as "the shipped 240-line `CLAUDE.md`", that is, by pointing at one, not by what makes it one.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — "index" is also used in a second sense, "the auto-memory index", under § What `/context` shows at launch, before the first sense has been defined.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § The window is a budget you can read states the demo's conclusion two units before the demo exists: "In our demo the manual changed none of the six behaviours we scored, so every token it cost bought no change in what the agent did; that holds on any window, which is why the argument for an index does not depend on window size." At that point the reader does not know what the demo is, what "the manual" is, what was scored, or that anyone has objected on grounds of window size. The sentence answers a question that was never put.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — the same sentence says "six behaviours we scored"; § The demo numbers seven "observables", of which 6 is "the control" and 7 is cost; the table groups "Observables 1–5" and "Control". Three names for one set, and the reader must work out that "six behaviours" means observables 1 to 6 and includes a control that was expected to fail in every arm.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` — § How a window fails uses the demo as evidence before it is introduced: "In our demo one convention ... was written nowhere, and the template's own sections modelled the opposite. It was followed in 0 of 15 runs". "Our demo", "the template", "the sections" and the 15 runs are all undefined here. The notes block then repeats the point and adds "the demo's control", a term not defined until § The demo. The example is also an odd fit for its bullet: a rule written nowhere and contradicted by every example in the repository is a designed-to-fail control, not the agent guessing, re-deriving or inventing.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` — the four bullets under § How a window fails are set in parallel but are not parallel. "Too little" and "Too much" are failure modes; "Position" is a dated qualification of "Too much"; "The bound" is the limit of the whole diagnosis, and its label does not say what is bounded. `topic.md` promises "two failure modes"; the slide shows four bold labels of equal weight.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` — the origin paragraph ("The name is recent. 'Context engineering' gained currency in June 2025 ...") closes § Why agents make the window the bottleneck and connects to nothing before or after it. `02-motivation.concepts.md` says the origin "explains why the problem changed when agents became multi-step"; the prose makes no such link. The spine names Lütke, Karpathy and Yan; the prose cites Yan and Willison. `01-context.concepts.md` rejected an origin slide as "history that explains no later choice"; the paragraph has the same fault at smaller size.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` and `sections/03-content.concepts.md` — the heading "The demo: one prompt, two windows" sits over a list of three arms, and the next unit reports three rows. The spine defends the heading on the ground that "This unit compares A with B. C is reported under § CLAUDE.md as an index"; the prose does neither, sending C to "the table under § What changed, and what did not". The spine's list of observables for this unit also omits the control. The spine records a plan the prose has left.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — the observables list under § The demo is written in the project's private vocabulary: "2 the sidecar in note form; 3 numeric order". "Sidecar" is never introduced to the reader, and "numeric order" does not say of what. The demo's question, the one line that makes the list intelligible, is in a notes block and so off the slide, while the seven-item list is on it.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — in the table under § What changed, "5 of 5" in the column "Observables 1–5" is ambiguous, because there are five observables and five runs. The caption resolves it only on a careful reading.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — § On demand, "**Here.** The `/round` skill's description loads at launch": the reader has not been told what `/round` is. § Pitfalls uses "a superseded run" and "a dry run", terms from the protocol that the prose never introduces.
- `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` — capability 1 points to "§ The window is a budget you can read" and not to § What `/context` shows at launch, the unit that shows the reading. "every run met observables 1 to 5 and built" says the build twice: observable 4 is the build passing.
- `sections/01-context.concepts.md`, `sections/02-motivation.concepts.md`, `sections/03-content.concepts.md` — "Overflow candidate" stands as an open question under four units (§ What this tutorial covers, § How a window fails, § The window is a budget you can read, § CLAUDE.md as an index), each with no decision recorded against it. The 01 spine still counts "a seven-step roadmap and the scope line on one slide" when the prose roadmap has six steps and the scope line has moved to notes. Questions that are flagged every time and closed never are an accumulating intention, not a plan.

State "163 tokens". Then re-point capability 1 in `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` to this unit. (*why:* capability 1 is "read the window and say what each part costs", and at present the tutorial shows the panel without once reading it; the reader is handed the instrument and not the reading.)
2. `sections/01-context.prose.md` § What this tutorial covers, and `sections/03-content.prose.md` § CLAUDE.md as an index — restore the gloss from `topic.md` to capability 2 at first use, and move the sentence "An index holds commands, conventions that cannot be inferred from the files, and pointers" out of the notes block to the head of the unit, with one clause saying what a manual is by contrast. Mirror the wording in § What you can do now. Rename "the auto-memory index" so the word has one meaning. (*why:* the reader is promised a skill named by a term that is defined late, in the document only, and never on a slide; every later use of "index" and "manual", including the demo's arms, leans on it.)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The window is a budget you can read — cut the sentence beginning "In our demo the manual changed none of the six behaviours" and place it under § CLAUDE.md as an index, after the table has been seen, which is where `03-content.concepts.md` already files the decision ("Scale ... answered by marginal value"). (*why:* the unit then does one job, the budget and the instrument, and the argument from cost arrives when the reader holds the evidence for it.)
4. `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails — reduce the demo reference in "Too little" to one clause with a forward pointer in the bullet itself, delete the notes block that repeats it, and do not use "control" before § The demo defines it. Set "Position" as a qualification under "Too much", and relabel "The bound" so that it says what it bounds, for example "When it is not a context problem". (*why:* the reader sees two failure modes and one limit, as `topic.md` promised, and is not asked to take evidence from an experiment nobody has described yet.)

Gloss "sidecar" and "numeric order" in the observables, and move the question out of the notes block to the top of the list. (*why:* a heading that miscounts what sits under it costs the reader a re-read, and the question is the line that tells them why the seven observables are there.)
