# Pedagogical Readiness — Round 2

**Recommendation:** needs revision
the claims are honest and sourced, but the reader is told what was done and what is documented, not shown how to do it: no fix is ever demonstrated working, and three of the four capabilities have no worked instance on the page

**Audience read against:** Graduate students in engineering and science, mixed disciplines, one month into a course on agentic development. They have installed Claude Code, run it in a git repository under a permission model, and edited a `CLAUDE.md` at least once. They know tokens and a finite budget, not attention. Nothing in the declaration says they have met skills, auto memory, hooks, MCP, path-scoped rules, subagents, managed policy, or headless mode. The outcome they are promised is operational: diagnose a context failure in their own session and fix it by changing what the agent sees or when.

## Strengths
- The audience declaration is specific enough to judge against, and the document's level matches it in register: no attention mechanics, no window sizes as a subject, token counts throughout.
- The four capabilities are stated once in § What this tutorial covers, repeated word for word in § What you can do now, and each is mapped to the unit that is supposed to deliver it. A reader can check the promise against the delivery without help.
- The null result is reported as a null result. The failed prediction is stated, the inference about the manual's 1,854 words is labelled an inference, and post-hoc observations are labelled post hoc. This models the testing habit capability 2 asks for better than a clean win would have.
- "Too little" is illustrated with a real case from the team's own runs (the unwritten convention, 0 of 15) rather than an invented one, and the third pitfall comes from two incidents the team actually hit.
- Evidence is cited only for what it tested: the length and position findings are dated, hedged, and explicitly not extended to agentic coding.
- `::: notes` blocks do their job. The slides stay within one idea each while the document carries the detail; the deck reads at 14 content slides with no visibly overfull frame.
- Spine and prose moved together since the last commit of record: `03-content` grew in both registers, and the spine records what was rejected and why. This is delivery keeping pace with promise, not a spine running ahead.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The demo, § What changed — capability 2 promises the reader can "test on a fixed prompt, over repeated runs, whether it changed the agent's behaviour", and the page does not let them. The prompt is paraphrased, not quoted. The command that ran it appears nowhere; "headless" is used without a gloss. `demo/PROTOCOL.md`, `demo/RESULTS.md`, `demo/run.sh` and `demo/prompt.txt` are never pointed to: the only two `demo/` paths in the prose are the index file and a screenshot. Neither the PDF nor the site carries a link to the repository, so even those two paths lead nowhere for a reader who was handed the document. The three lessons a reader most needs to run their own test (isolate the copy from git history, strip auto memory, fix the observables before running) are recorded in the protocol's amendments and absent from the tutorial. The reader learns that a test was run, not how to run one.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Pitfalls, with `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails and `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` § What you can do now — the declared outcome is diagnose and fix, and no fix is shown working anywhere in the document. The one failure the demo observed (the unwritten convention, 0 of 15) is left as a failure: the rule was never added to a loaded file and rerun, so the central claim, that changing what the agent sees changes what it does, is asserted and not demonstrated. The only behavioural evidence on the page is that a loaded file changed nothing. Diagnosis itself is given as a question ("ask what it could see, and when") and a bound ("if the agent had the information and still erred"), with no procedure for answering either: how does the reader establish whether the information was in the window? The first pitfall names two causes, too long or in conflict, and gives no way to tell them apart. The spine's own open question in `04-conclusion.concepts.md` ("Enough for the reader to do it in their own session?") has the answer no.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § What `/context` shows at launch, § Layers, § On demand — capabilities 1, 3 and 4 are delivered as reference lists with no worked instance, and the hardest material gets the least room.
  - Capability 1: the figure is shown and not read. The only commentary is one notes sentence about the Memory files row. Nothing says which rows the reader can change and which belong to the harness, or what the Autocompact buffer is. The rows sum to 65.3k against a headline of 32.3k because the 33.0k buffer is not counted as used; a reader asked to "say what each part costs" will add the column and be confused. In `output/document.pdf` the figure also floats to page 4, under the § 3.4 heading, so § 3.2 on page 3 is a sentence about a figure the reader has not seen.
  - Capability 3: "place an instruction in the right layer" is taught without saying where any layer lives. A reader who has edited one `CLAUDE.md` is not told the path of the user file or the local file. No conflict is shown, so "remove it" has nothing to act on.
  - Capability 4: "decide, for a given file" is never exercised on a given file. § On demand introduces `@path` imports, typed `@file`, subdirectory `CLAUDE.md`, path-scoped rules, skill bodies, `--add-dir`, commands and subagents on one slide, none with an example, and the distinction it most depends on (`@path` inside `CLAUDE.md` loads at launch, `@file` typed in a prompt loads on demand) is left for the reader to infer from two bullets. The one local example is a skill, not a file the reader would be deciding about.
  - Against this, the demo takes two slides and the largest notes blocks in the document to report a null result.
- `sections/02-motivation.prose.md` § How a window fails and `sections/03-content.prose.md` § The window is a budget you can read — the demo is argued from before it is introduced. § How a window fails cites "our demo", "0 of 15 runs" and "the template's own sections" two units before the reader knows there is a template or what was run; the forward pointer sits in a notes block, so the slide has none. The opening paragraph of `03-content` then uses "the manual", "an index" and "the six behaviours we scored" to settle an objection about window size that the reader has not raised, with all three terms undefined until § The demo. The first paragraph of the HOW section is unreadable on first pass.
- `sections/*.prose.md` — assumed knowledge is neither supplied nor named. Auto memory, skill (description versus body), MCP, hook, permission setting, path-scoped rules, subagents, managed policy, `CLAUDE.local.md`, "headless" and "sidecar" are all used bare. The declared audience covers none of them. Each is one clause to gloss; together they decide whether § On demand and the figure are legible to a reader one month in.
- `sections/03-content.concepts.md` § The demo and `topic.md` — spine and proposal have drifted from the prose in three places. The heading says "one prompt, two windows" over a slide that lists three arms and a table with three rows; the spine's reason for keeping the heading ("this unit compares A with B; C is reported under § CLAUDE.md as an index") is no longer true of the prose, and `demo/RESULTS.md` is already titled "three windows". `topic.md` § Shape and the spine both promise a "recorded diff" of outputs; the prose has counts and no output, so the reader never sees what a run produced or what violating the control looks like. `topic.md` § Open questions still lists "what else is loaded in both arms" as open when the spine records it as decided. In all three the prose is right about what was done and the other register should move, except the diff, where the prose should move.
